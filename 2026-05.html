Fort Worth, Texas Strategy Update

▾
🏗
Key Update — Construction Approach Revised
Following a thorough review of the permitting process with the landlord, and informed by lessons learned from the Denver buildout, the team has concluded that — given Fort Worth's build-to-suit nature — integrating MEP amenities (plumbing, electrical, ventilation) directly into the new construction yields a better outcome: lower cost, greater efficiency, and a cleaner final build.
Previous Approach
Modular Units
Prefabricated modular MEP installations. Greater complexity in integrating plumbing, electrical, and ventilation into an existing or partial structure.
Revised Approach
Built-in-Place
MEP systems designed and installed from the ground up as part of new construction. More cost-efficient and structurally cleaner for a build-to-suit project.
Since Fort Worth is a build-to-suit development, the complexity of retrofitting modular MEP systems is unnecessary. Building these systems in-place from the start is the standard approach for new construction — delivering a cleaner, more cost-efficient result from the outset.
→
Next Steps — Fort Worth
Advance construction planning with landlord and contractor using the built-in-place MEP strategy. Align on permit submission timeline and project schedule.

Dallas has been identified as an alternative to Austin and is now the primary Texas expansion focus. Material progress has been made with both the landlord and the general contractor on deal structure, budget, and design direction. Architects and engineers are being brought into the process.

Deal Structure — Build-to-Suit Financing

The transaction is a landlord-financed build-to-suit at 9910 Monroe Drive, Dallas TX 75220. The landlord (RAS Services, Inc. / Reynaldo Arizmendi) will finance construction of an approximately 23,000 SF facility — comprising 6 padel courts

Financial Analysis — Rent Economics

Arenna's base rent mirrors the landlord's annual debt service on the $2,000,000 construction loan. Total annual debt cost is estimated at approximately ~$306,000/year — which on the 23,000 SF building translates to roughly ~$13.4/sqft/year in base rent. On top of that, Arenna covers Triple Net (NNN) expenses: property maintenance, property insurance, and property taxes.

Preston Hollow is a premium submarket in North Dallas — a high-income residential and mixed-use corridor where industrial and flex space typically asks $14–$20/sqft NNN. This deal's base rent of ~$13.4/sqft is anchored to the landlord's $2M debt service, not to market comparables. The all-in of $16.90–$18.90/sqft comes in at or below the low end of the Preston Hollow market range.
💰
Deal Advantage
The landlord absorbs full construction and financing risk. Arenna's rent is anchored to a $2M loan — not to market rates — on a purpose-built facility. At ~$13.9/sqft base versus a $14–$20/sqft market, the deal creates meaningful structural savings from day one.
⚠
Risk — Floating Rate & Balloon Maturity
The floating rate introduces variability in the ~$306K annual estimate.

20×10 Initiative

Key Update — Lease Renewal Meeting Finally Held
After a prolonged period of delays by the Lienzo and Charro boards, the long-awaited lease renewal discussion took place at the end of May 2026. The meeting was an important step — though the initial position put forward by the Lienzo board fell well outside reasonable parameters.
⚠
Risk — Lienzo's Initial Proposal: Rent Doubling
The Lienzo board's opening position was a 100% rent increase — effectively doubling the current rent. This proposal was immediately rejected by the current owners, who made clear that such an increase would make the economics unworkable and would leave them with no alternative but to not renew and remove their infrastructure from the property. The proposal was deemed untenable by all parties with a stake in continuity.
